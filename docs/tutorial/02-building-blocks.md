# 2. The building blocks

[← What is Scenex?](01-what-is-scenex.md) · [Contents](index.md) · [Next: A tour of the editor →](03-editor-tour.md)

Every scenario in Scenex is assembled from the same dozen building blocks.
Learn these, and you can read any scenario — and write your own.

For each block you'll find **what it is**, **why it matters** on stage, and
**how Common Ground uses it**. 🪐 marks a peek at Mars Colony, where it does
something differently.

---

## 🎬 Scenario

**What it is.** The whole script of a megagame: its world, its groups, what
it measures, its story beats, its pictures and its endings.

**Why it matters.** The rules of play are the same for every scenario — the
scenario is where *your* story lives. That's why one platform can carry
Common Ground, Mars Colony, and whatever you dream up next.

**In Common Ground:** the town of Lux. An old hat factory, a mineral spring
underneath it, an IT company with an irresistible offer — and five groups who
must decide what kind of town Lux becomes.

## 🎟️ Session

**What it is.** One live performance of a scenario: one venue, one evening,
one room of players.

**Why it matters.** Every session is a fresh start and keeps its own record.
Ten theatres can play Common Ground on the same night without ever getting in
each other's way.

## 👥 Groups

**What it is.** The teams in the room. Every player belongs to exactly one
group, and every group sits together at one table with one group board.

**Why it matters.** Scenex doesn't track individuals — it tracks groups. That
keeps the game manageable with 60 people, and it puts the conversation
*inside* each group at the heart of the evening.

**In Common Ground:** five groups — Chamber of Commerce · Arts, Culture,
Sport · Customer's Protection · Voluntary Sector · Public Services.

## 📊 Values

**What it is.** The handful of things the game measures — the "pulse" of the
society. **Every group has its own number for every value**, within a range
you choose.

**Why it matters.** Values are how decisions leave a mark. They should mean
something the players can *feel*. Four or five values are plenty.

**In Common Ground:** Stability, Solidarity, Prosperity and Fairness, each
from 0 to 20. Every group starts at 8 in all four.

🪐 *Mars Colony uses Life Support, Supplies, Progress and Risk — and there,
for Risk, high is bad.*

## 🌍 Global values and their formulas

**What it is.** Besides each group's own numbers, every value has one number
for the **whole society**. Nobody types it in — Scenex calculates it from the
groups' numbers, using a formula you choose.

**Why it matters.** The formula is a political statement:

| Formula | What it says about your world |
|---|---|
| **average** | "We are all in this together." |
| **minimum** | "A chain is as strong as its weakest link." |
| **maximum** | "One troublemaker is enough to put us all at risk." |
| **median** | "One star (or one disaster) doesn't define us." |

**In Common Ground:** all four values use the average.

🪐 *Mars Colony mixes them on purpose: Life Support uses the minimum (one
failing habitat endangers everyone), Risk uses the maximum. One team that
lags behind drags the whole colony down — and the room notices.*

## 🏅 The Overall Index

**What it is.** One headline score that sums up how the society is doing,
calculated from the global values. You name it, and you give it labels from
worst to best.

**Why it matters.** It's the number the whole room watches. The projector
shows it as a dot moving between its worst and its best label — never as a
raw number, so people feel the trend instead of doing maths.

**In Common Ground:** the **Democracy Score** — the average of Stability,
Solidarity, Prosperity and Fairness — moving from *Breakdown* through
*Critical*, *Fragile* and *Resilient* to *In Bloom*.

🪐 *In Mars Colony it's called Colony Vitality, from "Barely Holding On" to
"Thriving".*

## 🙂 Well-being — a value counted in the room

**What it is.** A special kind of value that isn't calculated at all. The
game master simply asks the room — *"How are you feeling right now?"* —
counts hands for each answer, and types in the numbers.

**Why it matters.** It's a reality check. The numbers might say the society
is doing great, while the people in the room feel miserable — or the other
way round. That contrast is pure gold for the closing discussion. Decisions
can never change it; only the people can.

**In Common Ground:** **Well-Being**, on four steps: 🙁 Unhappy · 😐 Ok ·
🙂 Happy · 😀 Very happy. The *Happiness Survey* page puts the question on
the projector.

## 🧭 The timeline

**What it is.** The running order of the evening: the list of story beats.
There are three kinds — and each works at a different scale:

| Beat | Who decides | Scale | Whose numbers move |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Event** | every group, separately | the group | the deciding group's own |
| **Vote** | everyone together, one person one vote | the whole room | any group's |
| **Wildcard** | nobody chooses — the game master judges success or failure | one player | any group's |

**Why it matters.** The game master fires beats one by one, in whatever order
and at whatever pace the room needs — skipping some, holding others back.
The list is a menu, not a railway track.

## ⚡ Events

**What it is.** A situation that every group responds to **on its own**.
Each group gets its own set of options — usually two to four — and only sees
its own.

**Why it matters.** The same crisis is a different dilemma from every table.
The Chamber of Commerce and the Voluntary Sector face the same news, but not
the same choices.

**In Common Ground:** the Key Events. In *Positive Starting Proposal*, every
group decides what to build in its part of the old Hat Factory — the Chamber
of Commerce might choose a public market, the Voluntary Sector a community
pantry.

### Options and effects

Every option carries **effects**: small changes to the deciding group's own
values, like *Prosperity +2, Fairness +1*. Effects are added to the current
number, and a value never goes beyond its range.

### Deadlines and defaults

A beat can have a **deadline** — a countdown that everyone sees. Pressure
makes good theatre. An option can be the **default**: if a group hasn't
decided when time runs out, the default is chosen for them. (Not deciding is
also a decision — and the world makes it for you.)

**In Common Ground:** Key Events give the groups 5 or 6 minutes.

## 🗳️ Votes

**What it is.** One question for **the whole room**. Everybody votes — one
person, one vote — usually by show of hands. The game master counts, enters
the result and breaks ties.

**Why it matters.** This is where groups decide *about each other*. Each
ballot option has an **outcome matrix**: its own effects for every group. The
same result can lift one group and hurt another — that's what makes people
campaign, persuade and make deals.

**In Common Ground:** the Referendums — for example the *Social Media
Policy*: Ban, Regulate, or Educate. Each choice shifts Stability, Solidarity,
Prosperity and Fairness in its own way.

## 🃏 Wildcards

**What it is.** A secret mission or a special card for **one player**. Nobody
picks an option: when the moment has passed, the game master decides —
*success* or *failure* — and each outcome has its own outcome matrix.

**Why it matters.** Wildcards give individuals a spotlight, create subplots,
and give the game master tools to steer the room.

**In Common Ground:** the **Freeze Card** — a facilitation tool. If one
person dominates their group (or a group can't decide at all), the game
master hands someone the Freeze Card: that person may not vote or influence
their group for this event. Followed? Success, small bonus. Broken? Failure,
small penalty. And *City of Lux receives an investment!* — a tempting gift
with strings attached.

## 🔀 Two versions of one beat

**What it is.** The same story moment, written twice (or three times), with
different options — and the game master fires the one that fits the
evening so far.

**Why it matters.** The world responds to what the room has done.

**In Common Ground:** Key Events 3, 4 and 5 exist in versions for a higher
and a lower Democracy Score (Key Event 5 even in three). The game master
looks at the score and picks. Players never know there was another version.

## 🔒 Conditions

**What it is.** A rule that makes an option available only under certain
circumstances — for example *"only if your Prosperity is at least 5"*.

**Why it matters.** A locked option stays **visible, greyed out, with its
condition** — never hidden. "We can't afford it" is a scene in itself.

**In Common Ground:** not used (yet).

🪐 *Mars Colony uses it: Engineering may only build a battery bank if its
Supplies are 5 or more.*

## 🏷️ Labels

**What it is.** Small coloured tags on options, like *Cautious* or *Bold*.
They change nothing in the numbers.

**Why it matters.** They help a table read its options at a glance — and help
you check your own design ("does every group have a cautious choice?").

🪐 *Common Ground doesn't use labels; Mars Colony tags its options 🛡️
Cautious, 🚀 Bold and 🤝 Team Player.*

## 🖼️ Pages

**What it is.** Full-screen slides for the projector: a title, a welcome, a
survey question, an act break, a finale. The game master can put one up at
any time; the game keeps running underneath.

**In Common Ground:** *Common Ground Title*, *Welcome to Lux*, *Happiness
Survey*, *Survey at the End*, and three finales — *Final – Good / Medium / Bad
Ending*, each a full-screen picture.

## 🎭 Endings

**What it is.** The possible final scenes, each with a title and a text. An
ending can carry a **condition** on the final numbers; when the game ends,
Scenex **recommends** the endings that fit — and the game master picks.

**In Common Ground:** the three finales are pages, chosen by the game master.

🪐 *Mars Colony uses real endings: "The Colony Holds", "Getting By" and "On
the Brink", recommended by its score.*

## 📝 Director's notes

**What it is.** Notes for the game master and the performers, attached to
nearly everything — the scenario, groups, beats, options, endings. Players
never see them.

**Why it matters.** This is where authors talk to the people running the
show: staging, timing, delivery, "if the room is flat, skip to the vote".

**In Common Ground:** "Candidates swap groups.", "Part 1: on paper — Part 2:
digital", "Use when needed."

## 🌐 Languages

**What it is.** Everything the players see — names, titles, stories, options,
pages — can exist in several languages at once. Each session plays in one
language.

**Why it matters.** One shared Common Ground, played in Portuguese, Estonian,
Hungarian, Polish… without copying the scenario. If a text isn't translated
yet, the original language shows instead — never a blank.

## 🔖 Keys

**What it is.** Every piece of content also has a **key** — a short,
internal name such as `Key Event 1`. Keys are for authors and the game
master, are never shown to the audience, and are **never translated**.
A value's key is special: formulas use it, so it is written like
`stability` — lowercase, no spaces.

**Why it matters.** Keys are how everyone working on a shared scenario
finds their way around. **Keep them in English**, even when you translate —
see [Your Common Ground](08-your-common-ground.md).
